# Arabic Patient Content Style Guide

## Purpose

Arabic patient content is not a translation of the pharmacist notes.

It contains only the information that changes what the patient should do, avoid, recognize, or report.

## Language

- Use simple modern Arabic.
- Use short direct sentences.
- Use one idea per sentence.
- Prefer common words over clinical terminology.
- Keep the Arabic text RTL.
- Do not mix English words into Arabic sentences unless the medicine or device name genuinely needs to remain in its standard form.
- Units such as mg, mL and mcg may remain in standard scientific notation.

## Patient Counseling block

The counseling block may contain a little more explanation than the card, but it must still stay selective.

Typical content:

- why the medicine is used,
- how to use it,
- food/timing when it matters,
- the most important common actionable problem,
- a serious warning only when the patient needs to recognize it,
- missed-dose advice when useful,
- storage or device technique when it changes use.

Do not provide exhaustive adverse-effect lists.

## Patient Card

The card is more focused than the full counseling block.

Only show sections that contain useful content.

Preferred optional sections:

- لماذا أستخدمه؟
- كيف أستخدمه؟
- متى أستخدمه؟
- أهم تنبيه
- إذا نسيت الجرعة
- متى أطلب المساعدة؟

A card should usually contain three to five short sections, not every possible section.

## Writing examples

Prefer:

- خذ الدواء في نفس الوقت كل يوم.
- لا تسحق الحبة إلا إذا أخبرك الصيدلي أنها قابلة للسحق.
- تمضمض بعد استخدام البخاخ إذا كانت تعليمات دوائك تطلب ذلك.
- لا تضاعف الجرعة لتعويض الجرعة المنسية.

Avoid:

- long translated medical phrases,
- mixed Arabic/English sentences,
- vague warnings such as انتبه للأعراض الجانبية,
- statements that do not tell the patient what to do.

## Red flags

When a serious warning is included, write the action clearly.

Example:

اطلب مساعدة عاجلة إذا ظهر تورم في الوجه أو صعوبة في التنفس.

Do not list rare serious effects unless recognizing them changes patient safety.

## Device counseling

Device instructions must be product-specific when priming, loading, dose counters, cleaning, shaking, breath technique or hold time differ.

Generic visual guides may teach the core technique but must not overwrite exact manufacturer instructions.
